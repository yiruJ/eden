# Eden Music Academy: Financial Situation at Current and Maximum Capacity

**Prepared:** 13 August 2026
**Scope:** Private lessons only. Ensemble, Music Theory, Orchestra and Outreach are not in the revenue model, but are discussed in section 6 as the main lever.

**Sources:** Teachers database (7 teachers, live pay rates), Student profiles (45 active students), Private Lesson Packages (73 packages, Feb to Aug 2026), Private Lesson pricing page, Business Context studio hours. Overheads and the 120-student capacity figure are as supplied by you.

---

## 1. The headline

Three numbers matter.

| | |
|---|---:|
| Net profit today (45 students, mostly legacy rates) | **-$54,900/yr** |
| Students needed to break even | **~70** |
| Net profit at full capacity (120 students) | **$56,400/yr** |
| Your 60% share at full capacity | **$33,800/yr** |

You are currently running at a loss, and the loss is structural rather than accidental. It closes at roughly 70 students. But the more important finding is at the other end: **filling the studio to its physical limit produces about $34,000 a year for you.** That is the ceiling of the current model, not a stepping stone to something larger.

The business does not have a demand problem at these numbers. It has a unit economics problem.

### Is 120 students physically possible?

Yes on total hours, and it is tight but workable on the hours families actually want.

Studio hours are 37.25 per room per week, so 111.75 room-hours across three rooms.

| Students | Room-hours/wk | % of all open hours | % of prime hours* |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 45 | 26.5 | 24% | 31% |
| 80 | 47.1 | 42% | 55% |
| 100 | 58.9 | 53% | 69% |
| 120 | 70.7 | 63% | 83% |

*Prime = weekdays 3:30pm to 7:30pm plus all Saturday, 85.5 room-hours across three rooms.

**63% of total hours looks comfortable. 83% of prime hours does not.** School-age students overwhelmingly want the 3:30pm to 7:30pm weekday window and Saturday. At 120 students you would need to genuinely fill the 7:30pm to 9:15pm slots and every Saturday hour, which in practice means either adult students, older teenagers, or accepting that 120 is closer to 100 in the real world.

Treat 100 to 110 as the practical ceiling unless you actively sell late evening slots.

## 4. Why the ceiling is low: teacher cost

This is the whole story.

| Duration | Price (blended) | Teacher pay | Your margin | Margin % |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 30 min | $50.50 | $30.00 | $20.50 | 40.6% |
| 45 min | $73.50 | $45.00 | $28.50 | 38.8% |
| 60 min | $96.50 | $60.00 | $36.50 | 37.8% |

**Teacher pay is 60.2% of revenue** on the higher tier, 55.5% on the lower tier. Four of your seven teachers are on the higher tier, and any new hire needed to reach 120 students will come in at the top of the existing band, not the bottom.

That leaves roughly 40 cents of every dollar to cover $78,080 of fixed cost before anything reaches you. Each student contributes about $1,121 of gross profit a year, so you need 70 students purely to stand still.

To produce a $60,000 share for you at capacity, teacher cost would have to fall to **47.3% of revenue**, from 60.2% today. On the 30-minute lesson that means paying $23.90 instead of $30.00, a 20% pay cut. That is not a realistic move with your current team, and attempting it would likely cost you teachers, which costs you students.

The July pricing analysis noted the same problem from the other direction: margin varied from 24% to 56% by teacher, driven entirely by pay rates rather than by what students were charged. The price rise has lifted the floor, but the structure is unchanged. **You cannot price your way out of a 60% cost of delivery on a business with $78,000 of fixed cost and a hard physical ceiling of three rooms.**

## 5. Three problems with the inputs

I have modelled what you gave me, but three of the figures will not survive contact with your accountant, and two of them are large.

### 5.1 Outgoings of $1,200/yr is not credible

$1,200 a year is $100 a month. That does not cover public liability and professional indemnity insurance, utilities, internet, Notion and n8n and Google Workspace subscriptions, payment processing, piano tuning, sheet music, printing, accounting fees, or any advertising.

You are running Google Ads. Your student intake data shows Google Ads as the single largest acquisition channel at 13 students. Ad spend alone is almost certainly a multiple of $1,200.

This matters enormously because it moves break-even:

| $11,200 | 79 | $46,384 | $27,830 |
| $21,200 | 88 | $36,384 | $21,830 |

| $41,200 | 105 | $16,384 | $9,830 |

**Action: pull the last 12 months of business bank and card statements and total everything that is not rent, teacher pay, admin or marketing wages.** Until you do, every number in this report is optimistic. This is the highest-value hour of work available to you right now.

### 5.2 Superannuation is missing, and $20/hour needs checking

I have added 12% superannuation guarantee on the admin and marketing wages, which adds $2,880 a year. If those two are engaged as employees rather than as contractors with their own ABN, super is mandatory and this cost is real.

Separately, **$20/hour should be checked against the current national minimum wage.** As at 1 July 2025 the national minimum wage was $24.95/hour, and it has been reviewed since. $20/hour is below that figure. It may be lawful if the person is a junior under 21, or a genuine independent contractor, but if neither applies this is an underpayment exposure, not just a costing question. Verify it with your accountant or the Fair Work website before the next pay run.

If admin moves to $25/hour, that is $2,400 a year plus super.

### 5.3 GST could halve the result

At 120 students, revenue is $338,000, far above the $75,000 GST registration threshold. Whether private music tuition is GST-free in Australia depends on how it is characterised, and I am not in a position to rule on it.

If your lessons are taxable and your published prices are GST-inclusive:

| | At 120 students |
|---|---:|
| Revenue | $337,984 |
| GST payable (1/11th) | $30,726 |
| Net profit | $56,384 → **$25,658** |
| Your 60% share | $33,830 → **$15,395** |

**This single question is worth more than every other item in this report combined.** Get a written answer from your accountant. If lessons are taxable, your pricing needs to be reconsidered, because you are currently absorbing the GST rather than charging it.

## 6. The lever that actually works: room-hours, not students

Your binding constraint is three rooms during a four-hour weekday window. The right metric is therefore **gross profit per room-hour**, not gross profit per student.

The ensemble program runs only two formats, quartets (4 students) and duets (2 students), in 45-minute or 1-hour sessions, on a 7-week commitment with breaks matching school holidays. That means **roughly 28 teaching weeks a year against 48 for private lessons**, and every ensemble figure below is annualised at 28.

| Format | Revenue/room-hr | Teacher cost/hr | **Gross profit/room-hr** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Private 30 min | $101.00 | $60.00 | **$41.00** |
| Private 45 min | $98.00 | $60.00 | **$38.00** |
| Private 60 min | $96.50 | $60.00 | **$36.50** |
| Duet, 45 min, members @ $30 | $80.00 | $69.33 | **$10.67** |
| Duet, 1 hr, members @ $40 | $80.00 | $65.00 | **$15.00** |
| Quartet, 45 min, members @ $30 | $160.00 | $69.33 | **$90.67** |
| Quartet, 1 hr, members @ $40 | $160.00 | $65.00 | **$95.00** |
| Quartet, 1 hr, non-Eden @ $45 | $180.00 | $65.00 | **$115.00** |

Three things fall out of this.

**First, all three private lesson durations are worth roughly the same per room-hour.** The 30-minute lesson is marginally the best. There is no economic reason to push families toward longer lessons, and the pricing change has already fixed the previous problem where 60-minute lessons were actively worse.

**Second, a full quartet is the only format that beats a private lesson**, and it beats it decisively: $95 per room-hour against $41, using the same room and one teacher. It also relieves the capacity constraint, since four students in a one-hour quartet consume one room-hour where the same four in 30-minute private lessons consume two.

**Third, duets destroy value at current pricing.** A duet returns $10 to $15 per room-hour against $41 for private lessons. The room is worth more empty of duets and full of private students. To match private lessons, a 1-hour duet would need to be priced at **$55 per student, not $40.** Either reprice duets or stop offering them.

The rule is simple: **an ensemble needs four paying students to be worth running.** At four it is the most profitable thing you can put in a room. At three it drops to $55 per room-hour, and at two it is worse than an empty room used for anything else.

A realistic illustration: six prime-time room-hours a week as filled 1-hour quartets adds roughly **$16,000 a year of gross profit** and serves 24 students in the space 12 would otherwise occupy, without a single new enrolment.

**The binding constraint is not economics, it is eligibility.** 36 of your 45 active students are Beginners, only 7 are at Grade 1 or above, and 16 play piano, which does not fit a string quartet. Filling quartets from the current roster is the hard part, not making them pay.

### 6.1 Four quartets on top of 120 students

Modelled as 4 groups of 4, one hour, members drawn from the existing 120, run over 28 weeks.

| | Base (120 students) | + 4 quartets | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | $337,984 | $355,904 | +$17,920 |
| **Teacher pay** | $203,520 | **$210,800** | **+$7,280 (+3.6%)** |
| Gross profit | $134,464 | $145,104 | +$10,640 |
| Net profit | $56,384 | $67,024 | +$10,640 |
| **Your 60% share** | **$33,830** | **$40,214** | **+$6,384 (+19%)** |

**The leverage is the point: payroll rises 3.6%, your share rises 19%.** Ensemble teacher pay is per group, not per student, so the third and fourth student in a quartet cost nothing to serve.

Range across formats: teacher pay rises $5,824 (45 min, Dylan's rate) to $7,942 (1 hour, high tier). Your share rises $4,320 to $7,728, the top of that range being four groups of new non-Eden students at $45.

**Two cautions.**

*Ensemble must be additive, never a substitute.* Per student per year, a quartet place is worth **$665** of gross profit against **$984** for a 30-minute private lesson, because ensemble runs 28 weeks and private runs 48. Ensemble wins decisively per room-hour and loses per student. If a family drops private lessons to join an ensemble, you go backwards.

*Half-full groups collapse the return.* At three per group instead of four, the gain to you falls from $6,384 to $3,696. At two it is $1,008. The entire case rests on filling every seat.

Ensemble is the best use of a room you have, and it is worth roughly $16,000 a year to you at full stretch. But prime-time room-hours cap it at around 10 groups, and even maxed out it takes your share from $34,000 to $50,000. **It improves the answer in section 7 without changing it.**

On eligibility at capacity: if the current grade mix holds, 120 students yields roughly 19 at Grade 1 or above against 16 places needed for four groups. That is feasible but with almost no margin, and it assumes the roster matures. At today's 45 students, with 7 eligible, four groups is not fillable from members alone.

One system gap: **only Dylan Lee has ensemble pay rates recorded.** The other six teachers are blank. Set those rates deliberately before approving groups, because defaulting to the private-lesson tier logic costs about $660 a year across four groups.

## 7. What to do

### Immediately

1. **Total your real outgoings from 12 months of bank statements.** Everything in this report is provisional until this is done. One hour of work.
2. **Get a written GST determination from your accountant.** Potentially a $30,000 a year question.
3. **Check the $20/hour admin rate against the current minimum wage, and confirm whether admin and marketing are employees or contractors.** Compliance risk, not just cost.

### This quarter

4. **Protect the 1 March 2027 migration.** It is worth $26,000 a year of pure bottom line and it is already committed and communicated. The fee-policy timeline has you reviewing the new-price cohort's renewal rate in November, which is the right checkpoint. Do not soften the increase unless that data genuinely demands it.
5. **Build the ensemble program to four-plus students per group, and stop running groups of three at $30.** This is your highest-return operational change and it needs no new families, since you can recruit from the existing 45.
6. **Set a floor on new teacher pay.** Every hire at $30/$45/$60 hardcodes a 60% cost of delivery into the business permanently. If you cannot hire below that, then growth to 120 students genuinely is capped at a $34,000 return for you, and you should know that before signing the next contract.

### Before committing to growth

7. **Decide whether 120 students is the goal.** At 100 to 110 students, which is the realistic prime-time ceiling, your share is roughly $20,000 to $28,000 before the outgoings correction, and less after it. Growing from 45 to 110 students roughly triples the operational load, the scheduling complexity, the payroll, and the churn management, in exchange for a return that a single additional teaching day of your own would likely match.

That is the uncomfortable conclusion, and it is the one worth sitting with. **The question is not how to fill the studio. It is whether filling the studio, under this cost structure, is worth doing.** The answers that change it are ensemble mix, teacher pay structure, or a second location that spreads the $50,000 rent across more room-hours. Filling three rooms with private lessons is not one of them.

## Assumptions and limitations

- 48 teaching weeks per year. At 44 effective weeks, revenue falls about 8%.
- Every active student takes one lesson per week. This reconciles with the Feb to Aug package data (599 sessions over 24.4 weeks against an average roster of roughly 25), but gaps between packages will make real cash lower than the run rate.
- Duration mix at 120 students assumed identical to the current 45-student roster.
- Package mix of 90% ten-week and 10% five-week, from 59 of 73 packages being ten-session plus 4 twenty-session.
- Teacher pay taken from the Teachers database at face value. Note that the July analysis found Dylan Lee's *realised* cost at $16.39 per 30-minute lesson against a database rate of $27.50. If historical payments have not matched contracted rates, payroll needs an audit and these figures shift.
- Legacy realised rates ($38.66 / $60.66 / $76.56) carried forward from the July pricing analysis.
- Admin and marketing costed at 48 weeks, matching the teaching year. Year-round engagement adds roughly $2,000 plus super.
- Outgoings of $1,200 used as supplied. See section 5.1. This is the weakest input in the model.
- No allowance for owner's own teaching income, equipment purchase or replacement, bad debt, or the 40% profit share going to the other party.
- No GST applied in the base case. See section 5.3.
- Ensemble figures use the $30 and $40 per lesson rates and Dylan Lee's $65 per hour ensemble rate from the Teachers database. Only one teacher currently has ensemble rates recorded.
