## 7. Four things that could move these numbers materially

I have modelled exactly what you gave me. Four inputs will not survive contact with your accountant, and two of them are larger than anything in section 6.

### 7.1 GST is the biggest open question in the business

Revenue is $104,772 today and $131,376 after the migration. Both are well above the **$75,000 GST registration threshold**.

You are already paying GST on the way out. Rent is quoted inc GST, parking is quoted plus GST. That means roughly **$4,079 a year of input tax credits** you can only claim if you are registered.

The question is whether your lessons are a taxable supply. GST-free education in Australia applies to approved courses delivered by recognised institutions, and private music tuition at a commercial studio does not obviously fall inside that. I am not in a position to rule on it and neither is any general guidance.

| | At today's roster, current rates |
|---|---:|
| Revenue | $131,376 |
| GST payable if taxable (1/11th) | -$11,943 |
| Input tax credits on rent and parking | +$4,079 |
| **Net GST impact** | **-$7,865** |

If lessons are taxable and your published prices are GST-inclusive, you are absorbing the GST rather than charging it, and your break-even moves from 64 students to roughly 81.

**Get a written determination from your accountant. This is the highest-value hour of work available to you.**

### 7.2 Outgoings of $2,000/yr is not credible

$2,000 a year is $38 a week. That figure has to cover public liability and professional indemnity insurance, electricity, internet, phone, Notion, n8n, Google Workspace, Vercel, payment processing fees, piano tuning, sheet music, printing, accounting fees, and advertising.

**You employ a marketing coordinator at $15,600 a year.** A marketing coordinator with no advertising budget is unusual. Your student intake data has Google Ads as the largest single acquisition channel. Ad spend alone is very likely a multiple of $2,000.

I have used your figure as supplied, but I want to be direct: this number is almost certainly wrong, and every result above is optimistic because of it.

**Action: pull 12 months of business bank and card statements and total everything that is not rent, parking, teacher pay or admin wages.** One hour of work, and it is the difference between a model and a guess.

### 7.3 Employee or contractor, for the two admin roles

I have added 12% superannuation guarantee ($3,120) on the marketing and reception wages. If both are genuine independent contractors with their own ABN, that cost comes off and net profit improves by $3,120.

If they are employees, super is mandatory and there are two further questions worth putting to your accountant: whether $200 and $300 a week is being paid against defined hours at or above the relevant award rate, and whether either is entitled to leave accrual. This is a compliance question, not just a costing one.

### 7.4 Car parking frequency

I have read "$120 plus GST" as **weekly**, matching how you quoted rent. If it is actually monthly, overheads fall by $5,280 and net profit improves to -$41,988. Worth confirming, because it is not a trivial swing.

### The question underneath all of it

The business breaks even at 64 students and returns roughly $41,000 to you at a physical capacity of 120 it will realistically never reach. **The question is not how to fill the studio. It is whether filling the studio, under this cost structure, is worth doing.**

Three things change that answer, and none of them are "more private students": the ensemble program (four paying students per group beats a private lesson more than 2 to 1 per room-hour, see `capacity-financial-analysis.md` section 6), teacher pay structure, or a second location that spreads the $38,000 rent across more room-hours.
